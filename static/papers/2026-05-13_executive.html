ScanNet++ — a binding-site model for hotpin

Replacing ScanNet inside hotpin with a GVP-GNN and continuous ΔΔG labels.
Author: Anji Liu Date: 2026-05-13 Status: architecture A/B complete · data work in progress
+0.20 to +0.31 AUCPR

over the strongest like-for-like ScanNet baseline (locally retrained, with MSA), measured on every PPBS test split with non-overlapping 95% bootstrap CIs. The architecture swap is the dominant effect; continuous ΔΔG labels are the next axis of improvement and are in progress.

1. Why this exists

Hotpin is a peptide-binder design pipeline. One of its early stages calls ScanNet (Tubiana et al., 2022) as a subprocess to score per-residue binding probability on a target chain. Those scores propagate into all downstream hotspot selection — so any systematic miscall there costs us later in the pipeline.

ScanNet is a well-engineered 2022 model with hand-designed spatio-chemical filters and a 4 Å contact label. The label is geometrically convenient but energetically lossy (3.9 Å vs 2.0 Å carry the same positive label), and the architecture predates the SO(3)-equivariant message-passing wave. The goal of this sub-project is to keep hotpin's subprocess contract while replacing what's inside it.

3. Reproduction is correct

Before claiming any architecture win, we re-trained ScanNet locally in both configurations (noMSA and with MSA). Our retrained checkpoints match the paper's Table 1 numbers to within ±0.012 AUCPR on every split — the data, training pipeline, and the chain-level weighted PR metric are all reproducing correctly. The gap to GVP is therefore not a bug.

Paired dots: paper checkpoint (hollow) vs locally retrained (solid). Vertical position is AUCPR; short connecting lines visualise the reproduction error.

4. Architecture swap, explained

ScanNet builds a learned local chemistry feature: Gaussian filters in an atom-centred coordinate frame, then pooled into residue features, then a small graph attention. GVP-GNN builds a geometric feature: each residue carries both a scalar channel and a 3-vector channel; messages combine the two with provably SO(3)-equivariant operations. The relevant invariance is built-in instead of learned, and the model uses richer per-residue state.

5. Continuous ΔΔG labels in progress

Binary 4 Å contact labels treat every interface residue identically. We want a label that reflects how much each residue actually contributes to binding energy. The natural surrogate is the per-residue alanine-scanning ΔΔG — predicted by Rosetta or FoldX — which is continuous, signed, and correlates with mutational tolerance.

Labelling pipeline. Rosetta (default) and FoldX are run as parallel engines on a shared interface-residue set; the engine bake-off picks one for the full 17 k dispatch.

Once labels exist, training is a near-trivial swap: same model, same data loaders, same training loop, only the per-residue target tensor and the loss change (BCE → masked Huber). The architecture wins shown above should carry over.

6. Drop-in into hotpin

Hotpin calls ScanNet as a subprocess and parses the resulting CSV. The new model uses the same CSV columns in the same order — verified in scannetpp.cli.predict. No changes are required on the hotpin side; only the script that hotpin shells out to needs to point at the new checkpoint.

The integration seam is the subprocess call site at hotpin/mvp/src/pipeline/scannet_runner.py. CSV columns and ordering are preserved.
