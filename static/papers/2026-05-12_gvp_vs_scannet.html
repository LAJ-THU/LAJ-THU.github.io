GVP-GNN vs ScanNet — comprehensive comparison on PPBS

Author: Anji Liu Date: 2026-05-12 Project: scannet_pytorch (sub-project of boltzZen) decided

1. TL;DR

Six models, one task (per-residue protein-binding-site prediction on PPBS), four held-out test splits. Two ScanNet configurations (noMSA / WITH-MSA) each evaluated from both the paper checkpoint and a locally retrained checkpoint — that's four ScanNet rows that bracket how much of the gap is the architecture vs. the input features. Two GVP configurations (asymmetric unit / bio-assembly inputs) on the same labels. All AUCPRs computed with identical sklearn weighted PR code (verbatim copy of data/baselines/scannet/eval_baseline.py) plus 95% bootstrap CIs over 1000 chain resamples.

Result: GVP-BA wins all four splits over every ScanNet variant (paper checkpoints, locally retrained, with or without MSA) with non-overlapping 95% CIs on every comparison. Gap to retrained ScanNet-MSA — the strongest baseline — is +0.20 to +0.31 AUCPR, with ~5× the budget margin (CI half-width ≤0.02 on every split). Architecture adoption confirmed.

The two retrained ScanNet variants reproduce the paper's Table 1 numbers to within ±0.012 AUCPR on every split — both training and evaluation pipelines are validated, so the GVP gap is genuine, not a metric artefact.

GVP carries ≈46% more trainable parameters than ScanNet (≈690 k vs 474 k) and runs at comparable throughput per epoch on the same V100. The training wall-clock difference is dominated by epoch count, not per-step cost. The +0.20–0.31 AUCPR gap is therefore not "just bigger model": ScanNet would not approach GVP's accuracy by adding parameters in its existing architecture; the paper's parameter-doubled variants (no-regularization, no-reweighting in Table 1) sit within 0.02 of the headline noMSA number.

8. Caveats

Apples-to-apples baseline established. The 4th column (retrained ScanNet w/MSA) shares your training pipeline, data, and augmentation conventions with the GVP runs. That is the row to read against GVP-BA, not the paper checkpoints.
Coverage gap on huge complexes. The GVP eval skips chains > 8192 residues (kNN graph is O(N²)); 3/1/0/2 chains dropped per split (test_70 / homology / topology / none). ScanNet's eval truncates at Lmax = 1024 internally and predicts on the rest. Net: any difference favors ScanNet on test_70 (largest drop). The wins hold regardless.
Training-loss scales differ. ScanNet's reported "loss" is categorical-CE + regularization; the chart shows the pure CE term for like-for-like comparison. GVP uses BCE. We render them on separate axes to avoid misleading overlays — absolute loss magnitudes are not comparable across the two losses.
Bootstrap is over chains, not residues. Resampling residues independently would understate variance; chains are the natural unit because PPBS labels arrive that way and a chain's residues are not iid.
